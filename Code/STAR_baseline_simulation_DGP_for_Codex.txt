STAR-CALIBRATED BASELINE SIMULATION DGP

PURPOSE

This document summarizes a baseline population data-generating process
(DGP) for simulations motivated by the Project STAR application. The
objective is not to reproduce every feature of STAR exactly, but to
preserve the main features relevant for predictive external validity:

1.  a mean school ATE close to 0.20 control-group SD units;
2.  substantial cross-school treatment-effect heterogeneity;
3.  low predictive external-validity R2 (R2_EV), around 0.05;
4.  the estimated systematic treatment-effect structure Gamma from STAR;
    and
5.  realistic distributions and dependence patterns for the observed
    STAR covariates.

The main simulation dimensions can subsequently vary J (number of
schools), N_j (students per school), and the magnitude of residual
heterogeneity Sigma.

1. MODEL

For student i in school j:

    y_ij = T_ij * tau_ij + x_ij' beta + w_j * delta + u_ij

    u_ij ~ Normal(0, sigma_u^2)

Individual treatment effects:

    tau_ij = z_ij' theta_j

Hierarchical treatment-effect model:

    theta_j = Gamma * h_j + v_j

    v_j ~ Normal(0, Sigma)

School ATE:

    tau_j = zbar_j' theta_j
          = zbar_j' Gamma h_j + zbar_j' v_j

where

    h_j = (1, H1_j, H2_j)'

    H1_j = prop_teacher_eth_cauc
    H2_j = prop_degree_master+

    z_ij = (1, free_lunch_ij)'

    zbar_j = (1, pF_j)'

    pF_j = school-level proportion of free-lunch students

Outcome controls:

    x_ij = (1, female_ij, age_ij)'

School-level outcome covariate:

    w_j = avg_experience_j

2. BASELINE POPULATION GAMMA

Use the STAR posterior means as the population value:

             h intercept     H1          H2

z intercept 0.152091 0.110434 0.103012 z free_lunch 0.058677 -0.065810
-0.067449

Thus,

    Gamma_0 =
      [ 0.152091   0.110434   0.103012 ]
      [ 0.058677  -0.065810  -0.067449 ]

The systematic school ATE is

    mu_j = (1, pF_j) Gamma_0 (1, H1_j, H2_j)'.

3. STAR-CALIBRATED SCHOOL-LEVEL COVARIATE POPULATION

Preferred baseline: sample the following vector JOINTLY, with
replacement, from the empirical distribution of the 79 STAR schools:

    (H1_j, H2_j, W_j, pF_j)

where

    H1_j = prop_teacher_eth_cauc
    H2_j = prop_degree_master+
    W_j  = avg_experience
    pF_j = school proportion free_lunch

This empirical-joint-distribution approach is preferred to independent
normal draws because it preserves STAR’s non-Gaussian/discrete school
covariates and their correlations.

School-level empirical moments across the 79 schools:

                         mean        SD        min        max

H1 0.0647 0.9979 -3.1272 0.6310 H2 -0.0653 0.9132 -0.3361 3.8622 W
-0.0043 1.0061 -2.3741 2.2001 pF 0.4725 0.2714 0.0152 1.0000 observed
N_j 73.01 23.78 34 136

Important empirical correlations:

    Corr(H1, pF) = -0.4526
    Corr(H2, pF) = -0.0747
    Corr(W,  pF) = -0.1966

Therefore, DO NOT simulate pF independently of H in the preferred
baseline.

4. INDIVIDUAL-LEVEL COVARIATES

FREE LUNCH

Conditional on the simulated school’s pF_j:

    free_lunch_ij | pF_j ~ Bernoulli(pF_j)

The overall STAR student-level proportion is

    P(free_lunch = 1) = 0.4830.

FEMALE

A simple baseline is

    female_ij ~ Bernoulli(0.4870)

independently across students and schools.

AGE

The standardized STAR age variable is discrete. Use its empirical
population distribution rather than Normal(0,1):

    P(age = -2.787070) = 0.001040
    P(age = -0.551523) = 0.757282
    P(age = 1.684024) = 0.235610
    P(age = 3.919572) = 0.006068

This preserves the observed standardized age distribution closely.

5. TREATMENT ASSIGNMENT

For a simple randomized-trial baseline:

    T_ij ~ Bernoulli(0.3)

independently of the covariates and potential outcomes.

0.3 is the STAR treatment probability for small class size.

6. CALIBRATION OF THE SYSTEMATIC ATE COMPONENT

Using Gamma_0 above and the empirical joint distribution of (H1, H2, pF)
across the 79 STAR schools gives:

    E[mu_j]                  = 0.189500
    Var(mu_j) = V_sys       = 0.008501
    SD(mu_j)                 = 0.092202
    min(mu_j)                = -0.074192
    max(mu_j)                = 0.490580

Thus the STAR posterior mean Gamma and the empirical STAR covariate
population already imply a mean systematic ATE of approximately 0.19,
very close to the desired baseline mean of 0.20.

RECOMMENDATION: Do NOT modify Gamma_0 merely to force the mean to
exactly 0.20. Use the STAR posterior mean Gamma as the baseline
population value. The difference between 0.1895 and 0.20 is negligible
for the purpose of the simulation and preserving the empirical Gamma
gives the calibration a cleaner interpretation.

7. TARGET R2_EV AND RESIDUAL HETEROGENEITY

Baseline target:

    R2_EV = 0.05

Definition:

    R2_EV = V_sys / (V_sys + V_res)

where

    V_sys = Var[zbar_j' Gamma_0 h_j]

and

    V_res = E[zbar_j' Sigma_0 zbar_j].

Given

    V_sys = 0.008501

and R2_EV = 0.05, the required residual ATE variance is

    V_res = V_sys * (1 - R2_EV) / R2_EV
          = 0.161522.

For the simplest baseline, impose

    Sigma_0 = sigma_v^2 * I_2.

Since

    zbar_j = (1, pF_j)',

we have

    E[zbar_j' Sigma_0 zbar_j]
      = sigma_v^2 * E[1 + pF_j^2].

In the empirical STAR school population,

    E[1 + pF_j^2] = 1.295952.

Therefore choose

    sigma_v^2 = 0.124636

and hence

    Sigma_0 =
      [ 0.124636   0        ]
      [ 0          0.124636 ]

with

    sigma_v = 0.353038.

This construction gives R2_EV = 0.05 by design.

8. IMPLIED BASELINE ATE DISPERSION

Under the preceding calibration,

    V_total = V_sys + V_res
            = 0.170023

and

    SD(tau_j) = sqrt(V_total)
              = 0.412339.

For comparison, the posterior mean school ATEs in the STAR application
have:

    mean              = 0.191182
    SD                = 0.434692
    minimum           = -0.762141
    maximum           = 1.376106

Thus the baseline DGP has:

    mean ATE about 0.19-0.20;
    cross-school ATE SD about 0.41;
    STAR posterior-mean ATE SD about 0.43;
    R2_EV = 0.05.

This is a close and deliberately simple calibration to the empirical
STAR application. The realized minimum and maximum ATE in any Monte
Carlo sample should NOT be forced to equal the STAR observed range; they
should emerge randomly from the DGP.

9. OUTCOME-EQUATION NUISANCE PARAMETERS

The uploaded calibration summaries do not provide posterior estimates
for beta, delta, or sigma_u. Therefore these values should NOT be
described as STAR posterior estimates.

For a transparent baseline simulation, use:

    beta_0  = (0, 0, 0)'
    delta_0 = 0
    sigma_u = 1

so that

    y_ij = T_ij * tau_ij + u_ij,
    u_ij ~ Normal(0,1),

conditional on the included covariates.

This isolates the treatment-effect hierarchy, which is the main object
of the simulation. If posterior estimates of beta, delta, and sigma_u
from the STAR application are available, they can later replace these
nuisance values.

10. BASELINE SAMPLE SIZE

Suggested baseline:

    J   = 40 schools
    N_j = 200 students per school

Main comparative designs:

    J in {10, 20, 40, 80}

and, if studying the distinction between within-study information and
the number of studies,

    N_j in {50, 200, 1000}.

A useful main design changes one dimension at a time around J=40,
N_j=200.

11. BASELINE DGP: PROGRAMMING ALGORITHM

For each Monte Carlo replication:

A. For j = 1,…,J:

1.  Draw one row, with replacement, from the empirical 79-school
    distribution of

        (H1, H2, W, pF).

2.  Define

        h_j = (1, H1_j, H2_j)'.

3.  Draw

        v_j ~ MVN(0, Sigma_0).

4.  Set

        theta_j = Gamma_0 h_j + v_j.

B. For i = 1,…,N_j:

5.  Draw

        free_lunch_ij ~ Bernoulli(pF_j)
        female_ij     ~ Bernoulli(0.4870)
        age_ij        ~ empirical STAR age distribution
        T_ij          ~ Bernoulli(0.3).

6.  Define

        z_ij = (1, free_lunch_ij)'
        x_ij = (1, female_ij, age_ij)'.

7.  Compute

        tau_ij = z_ij' theta_j.

8.  Draw

        u_ij ~ Normal(0,1).

9.  Generate

        y_ij = T_ij*tau_ij + x_ij'beta_0 + W_j*delta_0 + u_ij.

C. Store the TRUE school ATE.

For the realized simulated school population,

    tau_j^true = zbar_j' theta_j,

where

    zbar_j = (1, mean_i(free_lunch_ij))'.

Alternatively, for the population ATE conditional on pF_j, use

    tau_j^pop = (1, pF_j)' theta_j.

Keep these two objects distinct in the code.

12. CORE BASELINE POPULATION VALUES

Use these as the default constants in the simulation code:

    Gamma_0 =
      [ 0.152091   0.110434   0.103012 ]
      [ 0.058677  -0.065810  -0.067449 ]

    Sigma_0 =
      [ 0.124636   0        ]
      [ 0          0.124636 ]

    R2_EV target = 0.05

    beta_0  = (0, 0, 0)'
    delta_0 = 0
    sigma_u = 1

    P(female=1) = 0.486997

    T_ij ~ Bernoulli(0.3)

    J_baseline   = 40
    N_j_baseline = 200

13. IMPORTANT SIMULATION CHECKS

Before running the full Monte Carlo experiment, simulate a very large
number of schools and verify numerically that:

    E(tau_j) is approximately 0.19-0.20;

    Var(systematic ATE) is approximately 0.008501;

    E(residual ATE variance) is approximately 0.161522;

    R2_EV is approximately 0.05;

    SD(tau_j) is approximately 0.412.

Also verify that the simulated joint covariate distribution preserves
the important STAR dependence between H1 and pF.

Do not calibrate the simulation by forcing each finite Monte Carlo
sample to have exactly these moments. These are POPULATION calibration
targets; finite samples should display sampling variation.

14. MAIN INTERPRETATION

The baseline DGP is designed so that observed individual- and
school-level effect modifiers generate a modest systematic component of
cross-school treatment-effect variation, while most predictive
uncertainty comes from the unobserved school-specific heterogeneity v_j.

This makes the simulation directly useful for illustrating the paper’s
main distinction:

-   increasing N_j improves learning about treatment effects in observed
    schools;
-   increasing J improves learning about Gamma and Sigma;
-   residual heterogeneity Sigma_0 creates irreducible predictive
    uncertainty for the treatment effect in a new school.

The baseline is intentionally STAR-calibrated but parsimonious enough
that the mechanism remains transparent.
